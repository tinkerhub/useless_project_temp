# AUR/S — Internal Product Concept Reference

**Audience:** AI agents and implementers.  
**Current stage:** Stage 0 — concept agreed; nothing described here has been implemented yet.  
**Purpose:** Captures the original creative direction and product constraints behind the formal PRD/TRD.  
**Update this file when:** the user changes the intended experience, aura rules, feature boundaries, or event constraints. Keep formal requirement IDs in [docs/PRD.md](docs/PRD.md) and implementation facts in [docs/TRD.md](docs/TRD.md).

## Status

This is the rough implementation plan for the agreed mobile/server project.
It keeps the previously agreed features and applies only these two changes:

1. Every subject receives a predetermined random aura range. Their live aura fluctuates inside that range. Bless and curse modifiers are also random and can produce milestone values, huge values, `+∞`, or `-∞`.
2. Aura interference is postponed until the normal scanner works. When added, it is probability-gated so accidental triggers do not dominate the demo.

The project is a mobile app, not a fixed installation. The Android phone streams its camera over the local Wi-Fi network to a friend’s PC. The PC runs the vision model and sends compact tracking data back to the phone.

### Chosen demo machine

Use the **RTX 4060 + Ryzen 9 PC** as the primary inference server. Keep the RTX 5050 + Ryzen 7 PC configured as a hot spare, not as a second stage in the live pipeline. Splitting a single camera feed across both machines would add network latency and make tracking IDs less reliable.

## One-line pitch

Point your phone at a crowd, let the app assign everyone a secret aura range, then tap people to reveal their live `AUR/s` and double-tap to expose their completely unverifiable instantaneous aura.

## Core experience

```text
Open Android app
        ↓
Point camera at a crowd
        ↓
Up to six visible people receive Subject IDs and aura fields
        ↓
Tap one person
        ↓
Their live AUR/s appears above their head
        ↓
Double-tap the selected person
        ↓
Aura scan reveals a signed result, modifier, label, and effects
```

The app never pretends that movement determines aura. Vision is used to find, track, and select people. The aura values are generated by the app’s random aura system.

## Final features

### 1. Mobile crowd scanner

The Android app shows the live camera preview and sends camera frames to the PC on the same Wi-Fi network.

The PC returns subject positions, simplified silhouettes, IDs, aura profiles, and scan state. The phone renders the visible aura effects locally.

### 2. Multi-person tracking

Track a maximum of six clear, sufficiently large people in a frame. Smaller or heavily occluded people can contribute to a background crowd count but do not need full aura overlays.

Each continuously tracked person receives a temporary label:

```text
SUBJECT 01
SUBJECT 04
SUBJECT 06
```

The ID is a visual session ID, not face recognition. If a person leaves the frame and later returns, they may receive a new ID.

### 3. Aura field from first detection

Every detected subject immediately receives an animated aura around their silhouette:

- coloured glow
- soft expanding field
- noise and particles
- colour and intensity based on the assigned aura profile
- subject ID above the head

The aura exists before the person is tapped.

### 4. Predetermined random aura profile

When a track is created, the server assigns it a profile once:

```text
Subject ID: 04
Range: 5,000–100,000 AUR/s
Seed: hidden
Type: CHAOTIC
Colour: magenta
```

The profile is stable for the lifetime of that subject track. The displayed value fluctuates randomly inside the assigned range.

Suggested bands:

| Band | Range | Suggested probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quiet | `0–100 AUR/s` | 40% |
| Noticeable | `500–1,000 AUR/s` | 30% |
| Powerful | `5,000–100,000 AUR/s` | 20% |
| Absurd | `100,000–1,000,000 AUR/s` | 9% |
| Impossible | `∞ AUR/s` | 1% |

The probability table is configuration, not a feature that needs to be exposed to users.

The gaps between ranges are intentional. A subject moving from `98 AUR/s` to another person showing `67696969 AUR/s` feels more surprising than a smooth universal scale.

### 5. Single-tap live reading

Only one subject can be actively monitored at a time.

When a user taps a person:

- that person becomes selected
- their aura brightens
- their live reading appears above their head
- the previous subject’s numeric reading disappears

Example:

```text
SUBJECT 04
18,472 AUR/s
```

The number should fluctuate continuously within that subject’s assigned band:

```text
18,472 → 18,519 → 18,103 → 19,002 → 18,777
```

Use a smooth random walk and digit-glitch animation rather than replacing the entire value with an unrelated random number every frame.

### 6. Double-tap instantaneous aura scan

Double-tapping the selected subject starts a short scan:

```text
SCANNING SUBJECT 04...
CALIBRATING AURA...
```

The scan reveals a signed instantaneous result:

```text
INSTANT AURA
+742 AUR
```

Possible results include:

```text
+184 AUR
-1,042 AUR
+4,999 AUR
+67,696,969 AUR
+∞ AUR
-∞ AUR
```

Positive and negative values must be formatted explicitly. The live `AUR/s` value remains non-negative; signed values belong to the instantaneous scan and modifiers.

### 7. Random blessing and curse modifiers

Bless and curse results are not manually chosen and are not based on movement. They are another random event generated during the scan.

The result card separates the components:

```text
BASE AURA:       +742 AUR
RANDOM BLESSING: +250 AUR
FINAL AURA:      +992 AUR
```

Milestones should have elevated probability compared with ordinary random numbers. Example milestone pool:

```text
69
67
420
1337
6969
67696969
676767
100000
1000000
```

Suggested scan distribution:

| Result type | Suggested probability |
|---|---:|
| Ordinary finite signed value | 55% |
| Recognisable milestone number | 30% |
| Extreme finite value | 12% |
| `+∞` | 1.5% |
| `-∞` | 1.5% |

Infinity is a special state, not a number to add to with normal arithmetic. If a subject receives infinity, display the modifier separately:

```text
BASE AURA: ∞
CURSE: -1,000 AUR
FINAL AURA: ∞
```

### 8. Scan effects

The effect is selected from the final result:

- small positive: warm green/gold glow and soft chime
- large positive: bloom, upward particles, bright pulse
- small negative: blue/purple distortion and low beep
- large negative: dark smoke, chromatic aberration, warning tone
- milestone number: special number reveal and celebratory effect
- `+∞`: white-hot field, screen bloom, `AURA OVERFLOW`
- `-∞`: black/purple collapse, low rumble, `NEGATIVE AURA SINGULARITY`

### 9. Sound and haptics

The selected subject’s live `AUR/s` controls a local Geiger-style sound. Higher values mean faster beeps and a higher pitch.

The phone can vibrate on:

- scan completion
- extreme value
- infinity result
- later interference event

Audio and vibration are generated on the phone, not sent from the PC.

### 10. Aura interference — post-MVP

Do not implement interference before the normal scanner works.

After the core setup is stable, add interference when two tracked subjects are near each other. Proximity only makes the pair eligible; it does not guarantee an event.

Use a gated probability such as:

```text
eligible pair + 5% chance per check
```

To prevent repeated accidental triggers:

- check each pair at most once every 2 seconds
- enforce a global interference cooldown of roughly 10 seconds
- require the pair to remain eligible briefly before rolling

When it triggers:

```text
AURA INTERFERENCE DETECTED
FIELD COLLISION: 87%
```

Their colours merge, particles become unstable, and the Geiger sound temporarily spikes.

## UI direction: fun first

The app should feel like a ridiculous handheld scientific instrument, not a normal computer-vision dashboard. The interface is part of the joke and should be immediately enjoyable even before anyone understands how the model works.

### Visual language

- dark instrument-panel background with neon green, cyan, purple, orange, and white-hot accents
- large playful monospace numbers for `AUR/s`
- fake technical labels such as `FIELD FLUX`, `SIGNAL CONFIDENCE`, `SUBJECT LOCK`, and `AURA STATUS`
- small animated scan lines, warning triangles, flickering indicators, and signal bars
- thick subject outlines and soft glowing aura fields that remain readable over a busy crowd
- colour-coded subject IDs so people can quickly identify themselves

### Interaction feedback

- subject labels appear with a short “lock-on” animation when first detected
- tapping a subject creates a targeting reticle and makes only that subject’s number prominent
- the live number changes with subtle digit scrambling rather than abruptly jumping
- double-tapping triggers a dramatic scan sequence with a progress ring, fake calibration text, and a final reveal
- positive aura results bloom outward; negative results distort, darken, or glitch the field
- milestone values receive special animations instead of using the normal result effect
- `+∞` becomes a white-hot overexposure effect; `-∞` becomes a dark collapsing-vortex effect
- the selected subject’s aura remains bright while other subjects become slightly dimmer, keeping the screen understandable

### Sound and haptics as UI

The Geiger sound should visually and audibly match the current state:

- low reading: slow, quiet beeps
- high reading: rapid, bright beeps
- scan complete: short confirmation pulse
- milestone: celebratory sound
- infinity: unique alarm or singularity sound
- interference: layered, unstable beeps

Use short sounds and restrained vibration so the app stays fun in a crowded venue instead of becoming noisy immediately.

### UI layout

Keep the live camera view dominant. Use a compact overlay rather than covering the screen with panels:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ AURA RADIATION MONITOR     LINK: OK  │
│                                      │
│       SUBJECT 04                     │
│    18,472 AUR/s (shows on one click) │
│          glowing aura                │
│                                      │
│                                      │
│                                      │
│                                      │
│ FIELD: 8,421 AUR/s        ACTIVE: 5  │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The design should remain legible in portrait mode and under inconsistent venue lighting. Effects can be dramatic, but the subject ID, selected reading, and scan state must always be readable.

### Fun details that are cheap to implement

- rotating fake unit labels such as `AUR/s`, `VIBE/m²`, and `FIELD INDEX`
- randomly generated warnings such as `UNLICENSED NEGATIVITY`, `SOCIAL OVEREXPOSURE`, and `UNSTABLE HUMAN SIGNAL`
- a tiny “scientist confidence” percentage that is deliberately overconfident
- scan result cards with fake classifications such as `RADIANT`, `VOID-ADJACENT`, and `SUSPICIOUSLY NORMAL`
- small loading messages such as `NEGOTIATING WITH THE FIELD` and `ASKING THE AURA NICELY`
- an optional shareable result card after a scan

The visual effects should be generated locally on the phone from the subject’s assigned aura profile and scan result. They do not need additional AI models or server round trips.

## Random aura technique

The server generates a session seed when the app connects. For every new subject ID, create a deterministic profile from that session seed and the subject ID.

```python
profile_seed = hash(session_seed, subject_id)
band = weighted_choice(AURA_BANDS, profile_seed)
baseline = log_uniform(band.minimum, band.maximum, profile_seed)
```

For the live display, maintain a stateful random walk. For large ranges, update in log space so a `5,000–100,000` subject does not spend all its time near the high end.

```python
next_value = current_value * exp(random_step(-0.08, 0.08))
next_value = clamp(next_value, band.minimum, band.maximum)
```

Use a separate weighted generator for the double-tap scan and its blessing/curse modifier.

The phone can own the animation after receiving the subject’s profile. The PC does not need to calculate every changing digit.

## Model choice

### Selected model: YOLO small instance segmentation in TensorRT FP16

Use `yolo26n-seg.pt` as the Step 4 correctness baseline. It provides per-person masks, boxes, and confidence scores while minimizing latency during initial integration. Instance masks are what allow the aura to follow a person instead of being only a glowing rectangle. After the pipeline works, benchmark `yolo26s-seg.pt` as the intended quality candidate and export the selected checkpoint to a **fixed-shape TensorRT FP16 engine** on the RTX 4060 PC before the event. [Ultralytics segmentation documentation](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/segment/)

Use a fixed `640×640` inference input and batch size `1`. Warm the engine before the demo begins. Fixed shapes avoid runtime shape-switch overhead, while FP16 is the appropriate first performance optimization for the RTX 4060. [NVIDIA TensorRT performance guidance](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt/latest/performance/optimization.html)

Keep `yolo26n-seg.pt` exported as a tested fallback engine. Switch to it only if the `s` model cannot maintain the end-to-end latency target on the actual phone feed.

Do not run separate pose and segmentation models in the first version. Aura values are random, so pose landmarks are unnecessary for the core experience. Add pose only if a later visual effect genuinely needs body joints.

### Tracking

Use Ultralytics tracking with `BoT-SORT` because the phone camera is moving. ByteTrack is a simpler option for a fixed camera, while BoT-SORT supports camera-motion compensation. Start with ReID disabled; only enable it if real crowd testing shows repeated ID swaps. [Ultralytics tracking documentation](https://docs.ultralytics.com/modes/track/)

Reliability rules:

- confirm a new subject after 3–5 consecutive detections so IDs do not flash on screen
- retain briefly lost tracks for roughly 0.8–1.5 seconds, then fade the aura instead of deleting it abruptly
- select active subjects by detection confidence and visible area, capped at six
- give an already selected subject priority when the frame has more than six people
- temporally smooth each mask contour before the phone renders it

Recommended starting configuration:

```text
model: yolo26s-seg.engine (TensorRT FP16 on the RTX 4060)
tracker: botsort.yaml
input: fixed 640×640
batch: 1
max active subjects: 6
```

If `yolo26s-seg.pt` is not available in the installed package, use the smallest supported person-segmentation checkpoint that has been tested on the RTX 4060. Do not use a larger model merely to increase GPU utilization.

## Mobile technology choice

### Use native Android Kotlin

Do not use React Native for the first build.

React Native is good when cross-platform UI is the main requirement. Here, the difficult parts are native camera-frame access, frame dropping, local-network streaming, overlay rendering, audio timing, vibration, and Android lifecycle handling. Since the target is Android only, React Native would add bridge and camera-module complexity without giving us a useful advantage.

### Mobile stack

```text
Kotlin
Jetpack Compose                  UI and controls
CameraX ImageAnalysis             camera frames
OkHttp WebSocket                  local streaming
Canvas / custom Android View      aura overlays and particles
SoundPool                         Geiger beeps
Vibrator / haptic feedback        scan effects
```

CameraX should use a latest-frame strategy so stale frames are dropped instead of creating latency. Android’s documentation specifically supports `STRATEGY_KEEP_ONLY_LATEST` for analyzers that cannot process every camera frame in real time. [CameraX image analysis documentation](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/camerax/analyze)

Use Jetpack Compose for the HUD and scan cards. Keep the per-frame aura drawing in one custom overlay view or Canvas layer rather than introducing a game engine.

## Server technology and setup

### Server stack

```text
Python 3.11+
Ultralytics
PyTorch with CUDA
OpenCV
NumPy
FastAPI
Uvicorn
WebSocket endpoint
```

The friend’s PC runs the server and connects to the same Wi-Fi network as the phone.

The server must run one latest-frame pipeline. Never queue multiple old camera frames waiting for inference.

### Connection flow

```text
PC starts server on 0.0.0.0:8765
        ↓
Phone connects to ws://PC_LOCAL_IP:8765/ws
        ↓
Phone sends compressed camera frames
        ↓
Server runs YOLO + tracking
        ↓
Server returns subject metadata as JSON
        ↓
Phone renders the aura and effects
```

For the first prototype and intended event build, use JPEG frames over a WebSocket. Send only the newest frame and discard stale frames. This is simpler than implementing WebRTC and should be adequate on a good local Wi-Fi network at reduced resolution.

WebRTC is a possible later upgrade only if the measured JPEG/WebSocket path has unacceptable end-to-end lag. It should not be the first dependency.

### Frame settings

Start with:

```text
phone uplink: 640×360 or 720p JPEG frames
server inference: fixed 640×640 TensorRT input
inference: roughly 10–20 FPS
JPEG quality: medium
process policy: latest frame only
```

The phone does not need to receive the processed video back. It receives compact tracking metadata such as:

```json
{
  "type": "frame_state",
  "timestamp": 1710000000,
  "subjects": [
    {
      "id": 4,
      "box": [0.31, 0.18, 0.21, 0.62],
      "mask": [[0.32, 0.19], [0.37, 0.21]],
      "auraBand": "powerful",
      "auraMin": "5000",
      "auraMax": "100000",
      "infinite": false
    }
  ]
}
```

Use normalized coordinates so portrait/landscape scaling is handled on the phone. Simplify masks to a small contour before sending them; do not send full-resolution binary masks in the first version.

## Latency and reliability rules

The goal is current results, not maximum GPU utilization or maximum nominal FPS.

```text
Phone camera → queue size 1
Wi-Fi upload   → queue size 1
Server input   → queue size 1
GPU inference  → one frame at a time
Phone overlay  → render newest metadata only
```

If a newer frame arrives while an older one is waiting, discard the older frame. A 15 FPS overlay that is current is better than a 30 FPS overlay that is half a second behind.

Return the client `frameId` with every server response. The phone can calculate actual capture-to-overlay latency without relying on synchronized clocks.

Targets for the event build:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---:|
| Median camera-to-overlay latency | under 120 ms |
| p95 camera-to-overlay latency | under 220 ms |
| Inference and tracking p95 | under 50 ms |
| Active aura subjects | maximum 6 |
| Returned metadata | under 20 KB per frame |

Warm the TensorRT engine before the first public scan. Build the TensorRT engine separately on the RTX 4060 machine; do not assume an engine built on another GPU or driver stack is optimal for it.

## Minimal implementation order

### Phase 1 — server connection

- Start FastAPI/WebSocket server.
- Connect the Android app over the local IP.
- Send one compressed frame at a time.
- Return a basic acknowledgement and latency display.

### Phase 2 — detection and tracking

- Load `yolo26n-seg.pt`.
- Track only the `person` class.
- Use BoT-SORT.
- Limit active tracks to six.
- Return boxes, simplified masks, confidence, and IDs.

### Phase 3 — aura assignment

- Assign each new track a seeded aura profile.
- Return its range to the phone once.
- Render an aura for every active subject.
- Add subject labels.

### Phase 4 — selection and live reading

- Implement tap hit-testing against subject boxes/masks.
- Allow only one selected subject.
- Display their fluctuating `AUR/s` above the head.
- Hide the previous subject’s numeric reading when selection changes.

### Phase 5 — double-tap scan

- Add double-tap detection.
- Play scan animation.
- Generate signed instantaneous aura.
- Generate random bless/curse modifier.
- Display base, modifier, and final value.
- Add result-specific visual, audio, and haptic effects.

### Phase 6 — interference

Only after the normal setup is reliable:

- detect eligible nearby pairs
- roll the 5% event chance with cooldowns
- render field collision
- spike audio and show the warning state

## Scope boundaries

Do not add these to the first working version:

- face recognition
- permanent identity across sessions
- pose estimation
- custom model training
- an LLM in the live frame loop
- WebRTC before WebSocket latency is tested
- distributed inference across both PCs
- unlimited tracked people
- full-resolution mask streaming
- a points economy for blessings and curses

The project is already technically rich because it combines live mobile video, server-side segmentation, multi-person tracking, random profile generation, mobile overlays, signed scan results, glitch effects, sound, and haptics.

## Privacy and demo safety

- Do not record or store camera frames.
- Process only on the local network.
- Do not identify people by face.
- Show a visible “camera active” state.
- Use the PC’s local IP only; do not expose the server to the public internet.
- Pair the phone with a short-lived session token so another device on the venue Wi-Fi cannot send frames to the server.
- Show `VISION LINK DEGRADED` and fade stale overlays if the connection drops instead of pretending the last result is live.
- Check the Ultralytics license terms before publicly distributing a packaged app or hosted service.

## Final feature summary

The final app should:

1. Stream a mobile camera to a local PC server.
2. Detect and track up to six people.
3. Assign each new subject a predetermined random aura range.
4. Show animated aura fields from the moment they are detected.
5. Allow one subject to be selected at a time.
6. Show that subject’s live fluctuating `AUR/s` above their head.
7. Double-tap to reveal a signed instantaneous aura.
8. Randomly apply ordinary, milestone, extreme, positive-infinity, or negative-infinity modifiers.
9. Display cool effects based on the final result.
10. Add randomized, cooldown-protected aura interference only after the core scanner works.
